## What Was Done

IXO answered all four open questions. Three of them closed a risk without needing code; one was a
permission grant we should never have been carrying.

### The code change: two permissions the frame never used

`allow="camera; microphone; clipboard-write; payment"` → `allow="clipboard-write"`.

IXO: *"Entirely in the KYC window… Nothing inside the iframe ever asks for camera or microphone, so
you do not need to delegate those permissions to the iframe."* Start verification opens a top-level
window on their origin, and ComplyCube capture — including the QR hand-off to a phone — happens
there, prompting for itself.

So the frame was being granted two of the most sensitive permissions a browser has, on a money
surface, for nothing. `payment` went with them: it delegates the Payment Request API, and the
provider collects bank and mobile-money details on its own pages rather than through the browser.

**`clipboard-write` is deliberately kept.** IXO were not asked about it, a reference to copy is a
plausible in-frame use, and its Permissions-Policy default is `self` — so a cross-origin frame does
need the grant. Harmless if unused; a silently dead copy button if removed and wrong. Drop it once
STAGE shows nothing needs it.

### Mobile: closed, and it confirms the resume path from the other side

All three targets run the full loop on a Yoma-style host page — iOS Safari, iOS Chrome **and Android
Chrome**, which was the gap left open on 2026-09-18. The frame waits in the background while the
youth is on the sign-in page and has already moved on when they switch back.

A completed sign-in **survives a reload of the Yoma tab**: the session lives on IXO's origin,
partitioned to the Yoma site, so re-embedding with a refreshed payment URL lands the youth back at
the payout or KYC step with no second sign-in. That is precisely the path already built — profile
refresh → active payout → "Continue cash out" → `GET /user/payout/zlto`. Adrian's answer and IXO's
now agree, from opposite ends. A reload *mid* sign-in just means tapping Sign in again; the parked
code is single-use and expires in five minutes.

⚠️ The partitioning has a second edge worth knowing: the iframe and a top-level tab on IXO's domain
are **different partitions**. Signing in through "Open in a new window" does not sign the youth in
inside the frame. It strands nobody — they finish in that tab — but nothing may assume state carries
across.

### Popup timing: not a risk

Opened synchronously in the click handler, blank, navigated afterwards. That is the pattern iOS
Safari enforces and the one thing that would have made blocks unavoidable. Blocker row downgraded to
Low.

### In-app browsers: a real gap, and a decision for the owner

IXO are blunt: *"We do not support the embedded flow there and I would not try."* Facebook, Instagram
and WhatsApp webviews block or mangle new windows, and **some replace the whole view with the sign-in
page, which kills the iframe** — so this is not a blocked popup with a graceful message; it can be
the frame disappearing underneath the youth.

Their supported path is the **standalone page**: the same `paymentUrl` we already hold, opened
top-level, where their page uses a full-window redirect and never pops.

Yoma does not detect webviews. Today a youth in one falls back to our permanent "Open in a new
window", or to the equivalent link IXO render inside their page when the popup is refused. Both are
manual and both are imperfect in a webview that mangles new windows.

**No code written for this** — see Open Questions. It is a product decision, not a UI one, and it is
not a STAGE-testing blocker.

## Gotchas

- **Do not re-add `camera`/`microphone` "just in case".** If a capture step ever appears inside the
  frame, that is IXO changing the flow, and it should be a conversation rather than a silent
  re-grant on a payment surface.
- **Partitioned session ≠ shared session.** The frame and a top-level provider tab are separate.
- **A webview can destroy the frame, not just block the popup.** Any future detection work should
  assume the frame may simply vanish, so "the iframe is still there" is not evidence of anything.
- Everything from 2026-09-18 still holds: no `sandbox`, escape hatch permanent and never re-timed,
  `shouldCloseOnOverlayClick={false}` load-bearing.

## Open Questions

- **Detect in-app webviews and skip the iframe, or keep relying on the fallbacks?** Detecting means
  UA-sniffing (`FBAN`/`FBAV`/`Instagram`/`WhatsApp` and friends) — fragile, and it would send the
  youth out of Yoma entirely with `window.location.assign(paymentUrl)`, since `window.open` is the
  thing that does not work there. Not detecting leaves a chunk of the audience on a path the provider
  says is unsupported. Yoma's social referral traffic makes this material rather than theoretical.
- Does anything inside the frame use the clipboard? Determines whether `allow` can go entirely.
